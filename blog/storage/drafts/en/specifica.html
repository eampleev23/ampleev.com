After posting my first article about the Burn Down Chart among friends, the first question asked in the comments was: "It would be interesting to learn more about your experience with SAFe." Therefore, in this article I will try to reveal the specifics of working in SAFe compared to Scrum in our practice. I certainly do not claim to have the absolute truth and will welcome any corrections and clarifications from colleagues.

First, I will briefly outline the main points that, in my view, distinguish working in SAFe from SCRUM.

  • Increment in Scrum and Program Increment in SAFe
  • Number of interacting teams
  • PO - not a business owner
  • Fifth sprint for training

Increment in Scrum and Program Increment in SAFe. In fact, they are different terms, but it is easy to get confused because none of us uses the term "Program Increment"; everyone just says "Increment" - and that is already a different term used in SCRUM. But in general they are quite similar in meaning, in my opinion. In SCRUM, an Increment is essentially the current product plus the planned stories for the sprint in a completed state (the team's passed DoD). A Program Increment is a time interval during which the train (Agile Release Train) delivers incremental value to production. In terms of duration, for us it is 4 sprints of 2 weeks each for development and one sprint for learning.

Agile Release Train - is a team of teams working on a single value stream. We simply call it a "train", but according to the framework the correct term is "Solution". In our case, this rule is not fully followed and teams often work on different products from a technical standpoint, because most business features are implemented holistically and involve several products. That's the technical side. But I'm sure that some consider all IT systems as a single product of the company, and perhaps that is even more accurate.

Number of interacting teams - In SAFe, the number of interacting teams is significantly higher; up to 125 people per train is recommended in total. We have 3 trains and this rule is not observed in all of them; there are trains with more than 125 people. SCRUM does not describe how to interact with other teams, as far as I know, unlike SAFe, which has very clear recommendations on this. The most prominent artifact for this, in my view, is the Program Board.

PO (Product Owner) - not a business owner. There is a Product Manager - he works with the Business Owners of the respective business areas and essentially assigns features to the Product Owners. The Product Owner works directly within the team - breaking down features into User Stories (in our case together with the team), and not always doing so, but we are constantly making progress in this direction.

RTE (Release Train Engineer) - the train conductor. Scrum Master of Scrum Masters. He conducts the Scrum of Scrums (SoS) events for his train. He interacts with Product Managers at the feature level. He facilitates the PI Planning Event (PIPE).

Fifth sprint for training - yes, unlike in Scrum, an entire sprint at the end of the increment is allocated for learning. As a rule, in practice we use it primarily for attending internal training courses (including SAFe), but not only. We try to schedule private courses and meetups for this time whenever possible. We also have developers who expand their skill set during the 5th sprint so that it is possible to cover someone else's competency in the team (to provide backup if needed).

System Demo. In the result of the PI Planning Event (PIPE), each team has a list of objectives for the increment, and each of these objectives is assessed by the train's Business Owner on a 10-point scale. This is called Business Value. These goals are divided into primary and stretch goals. Primary goals are those in which the team sees no risk of achievement. Stretch goals are those with achievement risks. All goals are mandatory and the scope of work is planned to cover all goals. At the System Demo, the Business Owner accepts the results of the work for the increment. Suppose a team had 3 primary goals with scores 10, 6, 8 and 2 stretch goals with scores 10, 3. Let's say the Business Owner gave the following actual scores: 8, 6, 4, 2, 0. Now you can calculate the percentage of goal achievement: 100% is taken as the initial total of the points for the primary goals, in our case 10+6+8=24. The achieved points are counted including the stretch goals (i.e., the team achieved 20 out of 24), so the resulting percentage of goal achievement for the team will be (20 * 100) / 24 = 83.3%. A normal result is considered to be between 80% and 100%.

In all other respects, we work by SCRUM. Or almost in everything :) As for our experience, I can say that, from my point of view, evolution is evident. Over a year of work, the teams have grown significantly, becoming self-organizing; some teams, however, have fallen apart, but even that demonstrates a certain evolution. There is still room to grow.
